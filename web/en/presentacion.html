The problem

"What did you do at school today?"

Families ask every afternoon. For a child with autism, or a child who does not speak, that question goes unanswered.
Good day today. Ate well. Best, your teacher.
...

The teacher has a lot to tell but no time to write it down. And what does get written cannot help the child tell their day, or show how they are progressing.

Step 1 · the teacher

She dictates how the day went.
Two minutes, on her phone.

  • She talks the way she would to a colleague.
  • No forms, no checkboxes.
  • The teacher view is protected.

Which day is hardest for him?
Mondays, at long break. On the 12th, 19th and 26th the playground noise upset him and he calmed down with his headphones. At home you can practise with the "help" pictogram.
Should I give him ibuprofen?
That is a question for his pediatrician. I can only tell you what the school recorded.
Step 4 · the family

A private space,
no app to install

  • They see the child's days in pictograms and how the child felt.
  • They see which goals the child is making progress on.
  • They can ask Puente questions anytime, and answers are based only on what the school recorded.
  • It never diagnoses or gives medical advice.

Handled with care

This is children's data. Here is how we treat it.

PrivateEach family only sees their own child. The teacher view requires sign-in. Internal notes never leave the school.
No diagnosesPuente describes what happened and what works. It does not label, it does not prescribe, and it does not replace any professional.
The teacher decidesThe assistant suggests and shows where each suggestion comes from. Nothing reaches the family without going through her.
Low costIt uses inexpensive AI models with a free fallback: the cost per student is a few cents a month.
